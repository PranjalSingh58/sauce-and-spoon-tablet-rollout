
# Sauce & Spoon Tablet Rollout Project

## Project Overview

The Sauce & Spoon Tablet Rollout Project is a project management case study focused on evaluating the effectiveness of introducing tablets for customer ordering at two restaurant locations: **North** and **Downtown**.

The project involved planning and monitoring a test launch, collecting customer feedback, analyzing survey data, tracking project performance, and identifying improvements before a potential wider rollout.

This project demonstrates practical application of **project planning, stakeholder management, risk management, data analysis, KPI tracking, and project monitoring**.

---

## Business Problem

Sauce & Spoon wanted to evaluate whether introducing tablets for customer ordering could improve the customer experience and support restaurant operations.

Before expanding the tablet system, the project team needed to understand:

* How customers responded to the tablets
* Whether customers found the ordering experience effective
* Whether orders were accurate
* Whether the tablets created technical issues
* Whether the technology affected table-turn time
* Which areas required improvement before a larger rollout

The test launch was designed to collect real customer feedback and operational data to support the rollout decision.

# Test Launch Results

The test launch collected feedback from **50 customers** across the North and Downtown restaurant locations.

## Menu Item Selection

| Menu Item  | Customers | Percentage |
| ---------- | --------: | ---------: |
| Drinks     |        28 |        56% |
| Appetizers |        41 |        82% |
| Dinner     |        50 |       100% |
| Dessert    |        35 |        70% |

Dinner was ordered by all 50 participants, while appetizers were the second-most selected category.

---

## Tablet Usage

| Tablet Usage       | Customers | Percentage |
| ------------------ | --------: | ---------: |
| Used tablet        |        18 |        36% |
| Did not use tablet |        32 |        64% |

The results showed that tablet adoption during the test was relatively limited, with 36% of participants using the tablet.

This highlighted the importance of understanding customer preferences and identifying potential barriers to tablet adoption before a broader rollout.

---

## Customer Experience Rating

Customers rated their experience on a scale from 1 to 5.

| Rating | Description | Customers | Percentage |
| ------ | ----------- | --------: | ---------: |
| 1      | Lacking     |         2 |         4% |
| 2      | Mediocre    |         5 |        10% |
| 3      | Neutral     |         7 |        14% |
| 4      | Good        |        20 |        40% |
| 5      | Great       |        16 |        32% |

### Key finding

**72% of customers rated their experience as Good or Great.**

This indicates that the majority of customers who participated in the test had a positive experience.

---

## Newsletter Sign-Up

**78% of participants signed up for the newsletter.**

This provided an additional indication of customer engagement during the test launch.

---

## Technical Issues

**12% of participants experienced technical issues.**

Technical reliability was therefore identified as an area that should be monitored and improved before expanding the tablet rollout.

The survey data was analyzed to identify patterns in customer behavior and operational performance.

# Retrospective

A retrospective was conducted after the test launch to identify what worked well and what could be improved.

## What Went Well

* Tickets were easy to track.
* Customers generally became familiar with the tablets.
* Table-turn time remained stable.
* The test generated useful customer feedback.
* The majority of customers reported a positive experience.

## Areas for Improvement

The project identified several areas that required additional attention:

* Improve graphic functionality.
* Investigate why some orders were sent back.
* Establish a better process for adding new menu items.
* Continue monitoring table-turn time.
* Improve tablet adoption among customers.
* Reduce technical issues.

---

# Lessons Learned

The project provided several practical project-management lessons:

### 1. Data should support project decisions

Collecting customer feedback and operational metrics made it possible to evaluate the test launch using evidence rather than assumptions.

### 2. KPIs should be defined early

Clearly defined KPIs made it easier to determine whether the test launch was achieving its intended outcomes.

### 3. Customer adoption matters

Introducing a new technology does not automatically mean customers will use it. Adoption should be measured and potential barriers should be identified.

### 4. Technical risks require continuous monitoring

Even a relatively small percentage of technical issues can affect the customer experience and should be addressed before scaling.

### 5. Retrospectives improve future project planning

Reviewing what worked and what did not work helped identify improvements that could be incorporated into a future rollout.

### 6. Project plans should evolve

The project plan should be updated as new information, risks, feedback, and lessons are identified throughout the project lifecycle.

---

# Recommendations

Based on the test results, the following actions are recommended before a wider rollout:

1. Investigate the reasons behind the relatively low tablet adoption rate.
2. Improve tablet interface and graphic functionality.
3. Investigate causes of orders being sent back.
4. Reduce technical issues identified during the test.
5. Create a reliable process for updating new menu items.
6. Continue monitoring order accuracy.
7. Continue tracking table-turn time during future testing.
8. Conduct another controlled test after implementing improvements.
